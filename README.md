## How I Got Into Cybersecurity

I started my career in military intelligence, where my early work centered on imagery analysis, targeting, and collections management. These roles built a strong foundation in structured analysis and supporting high-tempo operations. At the time, cyber was part of the broader mission environment but not my primary focus.

The transition began as I moved into more integrated intelligence positions. I saw how traditional collection and targeting work increasingly overlapped with digital environments. A key shift occurred when I supported cyberspace-focused intelligence analysis, where I worked at the intersection of digital operations, open-source intelligence, and signals intelligence. This included network discovery, content and pattern-of-life analysis, and contributing to joint planning efforts. Supporting these activities showed me that the analytic skills I had developed in conventional intelligence applied directly to understanding adversary behavior in digital spaces.

From there, my path into cybersecurity became intentional. In subsequent roles I focused on foreign threat actors, advanced persistent threat tradecraft, malware and intrusion analysis, and producing cyber threat intelligence to inform decision-making. I regularly apply frameworks such as MITRE ATT&CK and the Diamond Model, along with tools for network, log, and malware analysis. Pursuing a Bachelor’s in Applied Cybersecurity, earning certifications including GSEC, GCIH, Security+, and CySA+, and competing as Team Captain in the National Cyber League Cyber Operations Challenge have helped formalize and strengthen the technical side of work I was already performing operationally.

What continues to draw me to cybersecurity is the same core interest that drew me to intelligence: taking complex, incomplete information, applying structured methods, and turning it into actionable insight—now directed at protecting critical systems and understanding sophisticated digital threats.
